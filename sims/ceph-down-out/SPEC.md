# ceph-down-out: spec

One concept: **down is degraded and nothing moves; out triggers rebalancing after a timer.** Task
`docs/tasks/w3/ceph-down-out.md`, catalogue `sto-ceph-down-out` (§4.6). Class stepper. The student stops an OSD and
lets time pass.

## Scene

- **Clock line:** `t = h:mm:ss` since the failure; osd.0's two flags as two badges (`up`/`down`, `in`/`out`), its
  process (running / stopped), and the cluster flag `noout` (struck through when not set). Under it the PG counts.
- **OSDs by host** (K3 block grid): 3 hosts × 4 OSDs, each cell the OSD id with its flags under it (`↑ in`, `↓ in`,
  `↓ out`, `↑ out`). up/in solid; stopped but still marked up, red frame (✕); down/in hatched; down/out grey crossed;
  up/out dashed outline. osd.0 carries the changed mark when its flags change.
- **Timers:** heartbeat grace (20 s) and down → out (the param), each a bar of elapsed time and a word: not running,
  `n` left, fired at, held (noout), cancelled (osd.0 back), not needed (drain).
- **Data movement:** a bar of the current plan, moved bytes by kind (recovery solid, backfill striped), with
  "moved of total · time left at rate"; "0 bytes moving" when there is no plan. Under it the bytes moved over the run
  and, when there are any, the bytes thrown away.
- **Every PG of the pool** (K3, 8 rows of 16): clean solid green, degraded hatched, misplaced dashed outline.
- **Six of osd.0's 30 PGs** (K3): per row the PG id, its three copies in CRUSH order (osd.0's styled as in the OSD
  grid, without the flags line), a fourth cell for the new place of osd.0's copy while it is out (dashed queued,
  hatched copying, solid done) or the temporary copy while a rebuilt PG is copied back, and its status in words.

## State

`{ t, osd: { 0: { run, up, in, downSince } }, flags: { noout }, timers: { grace, downOut: { start, len, state } },
plan: { start, items: [{ pg, to, from, kind, bytes, log }], total, moved, kind } | null, totals: { moved, wasted },
dataBytes, rate, pgs: [group per PG], counts: { clean, degraded, misplaced }, zoom: [pg], ev, i }`. `ev` is this step's
event with `changed` (PGs whose group changed) and `osdChanged`; `return` also carries `hadPlan`, `movedBefore`,
`doneBefore`, `wasted`.

ceph-min-size's `{ raw, down, counts }` is not reused as such: only one OSD changes, so its flags replace `down`, and
`raw` is the constant CRUSH output in `placement.js` (with osd.0 in and with osd.0 out).

## Placement

`placement.js` is generated by `tools/gen-placement.mjs` with the cephsim engine (`sims/cephsim/js/engine`:
`compile.js`, `pgmap.js` `mapPool`) from cephsim `samples.js` `hyperconverged`: 3 hosts × 4 OSDs, pool 1
`cephfs_data`, 128 PGs, size 3, `chooseleaf firstn 0 type host`. Twice: every OSD in (the same as
`sims/ceph-min-size/placement.js` `hc`, the generator checks it), and osd.0 out (reweight 0).

Checked: 30 PGs have a copy on osd.0. With osd.0 out exactly those 30 change, each by one OSD (osd.0's copy goes to
osd.1, 2 or 3: with three hosts and size 3 the copy must stay on ceph-01), and no other copy moves. So the bytes to
move are exactly osd.0's data.

## Rules

Sources: Ceph documentation "Configuring Monitor/OSD Interaction" (`osd_heartbeat_grace`,
`mon_osd_down_out_interval`), "Placement Group States", "Operating a Cluster" (`noout`) *(check: from memory, no
network)*; cephsim `js/engine/recovery.js` (`planRecovery`, `Recovery`), `js/engine/pgmap.js` (`pgState`).

1. An OSD that **goes silent** (its disk hangs, its host loses power) stays marked **up** until its peers have
   missed heartbeats for the grace period (`osd_heartbeat_grace`, 20 s), then it is marked **down** (*check*). Between
   the two the map, and so every PG, is unchanged. This sim's "stop" is that case. An OSD stopped cleanly tells the
   monitors and is marked down at once, and one that crashes on a live host is reported at once because its peers get
   a refused connection (`osd_fast_fail_on_connection_refused`, *check*); the explanation says so, the model does
   not step through it.
2. **Down but in:** each PG with a copy on it is **degraded** (2 of 3 live copies; still active, min_size 2). CRUSH
   still maps the PG to it, so **nothing moves** (cephsim `planRecovery`: "marking an OSD down queues no work at all").
3. After `mon_osd_down_out_interval` (600 s by default, counted from the moment it was marked down) a down OSD is
   marked **out** (weight 0) (*check*). CRUSH maps its PGs elsewhere (placement `out0`) and the work starts.
4. A PG short of a live copy gets **recovery**; a PG whose copies are all live but not where CRUSH wants them is
   **misplaced** and gets **backfill**. Recovery items go first (cephsim `planRecovery` sorts them first).
5. **Back before the timer:** marked up (it was still in); each of its PGs only catches up on the writes it missed
   (recovery from the log; cephsim has no PG log, so the size is an assumption: the pool takes 10 MB/s of client
   writes, spread evenly over its 128 PGs, for the whole time osd.0 was away, from the stop). The PGs stay degraded
   until the catch-up is done. **Back after it was out:** marked in again (Ceph marks a booting OSD that was marked
   out automatically back in, `mon_osd_auto_mark_auto_out_in`, *check*) and CRUSH maps its PGs back to it. A PG whose
   rebuild had finished is now misplaced and is copied back to osd.0 (backfill of its whole share: the data moves
   twice; that it is a full copy and not a log catch-up is a simplification, *check*). Every other PG catches up as
   above, and what was copied for it is thrown away.
6. **noout** stops rule 3: the timer runs out and nothing happens; the OSD stays in, its PGs stay degraded, no data
   moves.
7. **Drain:** osd.0 is marked out while it is up. Every copy is live, so its PGs are misplaced, never degraded:
   backfill only (cephsim `pgState`: remapped, not degraded). Once empty it is stopped: a minute after the last byte,
   marked down a grace period later; no PG changes.
8. **Time = bytes / rate.** A fixed rate (the param, MB/s = 10⁶ B/s), one item at a time in queue order (cephsim
   spreads it over per-host bandwidth × `recoveryFraction` 0.5 with `maxBackfills` 1; the demo uses one rate). Sizes
   are binary (TiB = 2⁴⁰ B, GiB = 2³⁰ B), as the cluster reports them. osd.0's data (the param) is split evenly over
   its 30 PGs.

**Tie-breaks.**
- A return at the moment the timer fires wins: the OSD is up again before it can be marked out.
- A progress mark (25 / 50 / 100 %) at or after the return is dropped. If none falls between the out and the return,
  a "moving" step at their midpoint shows the copy under way.
- Queue order: recovery before backfill, then PG number.
- An item is done only when the bytes moved cover it and every item before it: 25 % of 30 equal items is 7 done
  (7.5 covered), not 8.
- The zoom: osd.0's PGs at queue positions ⌊k × 30 / 6⌋, k = 0…5 (positions 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25), in PG order,
  fixed for the run.

## Steps

One step moves the clock to the next event and recomputes every PG and the plan.

- stop: `stop` (0 s), `down` (20 s); then
  - back no later than the out (or noout): [`timer` at 20 s + interval, only with noout and a return after it],
    `return`, `clean` (return + catch-up / rate); with noout and no return: `timer`, `later` (1 h);
  - otherwise: `wait` (halfway through the interval), `out` (20 s + interval), the progress marks before the return,
    [`moving`], [`return`, `clean`].
- drain: `out` (0 s), 25 %, 50 %, 100 %, `stopEmpty` (100 % + 60 s + 20 s).

Every parameter combination has 4–9 steps (tested).

| preset | params | steps | events |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dead-disk` | (defaults) | 7 | stop, down, wait, out, 25 %, 50 %, 100 % |
| `reboot` | returnAt 5 min | 4 | stop, down, return, clean |
| `slow-reboot` | returnAt 15 min | 7 | stop, down, wait, out, moving, return, clean |
| `maintenance-noout` | noout, returnAt 30 min | 5 | stop, down, timer, return, clean |
| `drain` | action drain | 5 | out, 25 %, 50 %, 100 %, stopEmpty |

## Params

`action` stop / drain (stop); `downOut` 60 / 600 / 1800 s (600); `noout` (off); `returnAt` never / 5 / 15 / 30 min
(never); `rateMBs` 100 / 200 / 500 (200); `dataTiB` 0.5–4 in steps of 0.1 (2.7). Every param resets. `downOut`,
`noout` and `returnAt` are hidden under drain.

| maintenance-noout | timer at 620 s held; 0 B moved for 30 min; back at 1800 s: catch-up **3.93 GiB**; clean at 0:30:21 (without noout: 220 GiB copied by then, 2 PGs rebuilt and copied back, 35 GiB thrown away) |
